## Summary

Flow matching trains a **continuous normalizing flow** — a neural ODE $dx/dt = v(x,t)$ transporting a simple prior to the data — by directly regressing its velocity field onto a prescribed probability path, avoiding the expensive ODE simulation that made CNFs impractical. The key trick is that an intractable *marginal* velocity can be fit through a tractable *conditional* one with the same gradient (conditional flow matching). Choosing straight-line, constant-speed conditional paths gives **rectified flow**, the basis of Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX. Flow matching turns out to be diffusion under a particular noise schedule and loss weighting, so the two frameworks largely coincide; its appeal is conceptual simplicity, an ODE-only formulation, and straighter sampling trajectories. It is the continuous-flow face of the diffusion subgraph.

## Open Questions

Whether **straightness** is the real goal is contested: rectified flow motivates itself by straight paths, but reflow-on-a-DDPM shows the essential ingredient is deterministic noise–data pairing (a consistent first-order ODE), not straightness or velocity-prediction {2024_Wang_RectDiffusion}, and flow-matching marginal trajectories are in fact curved because the velocity regresses a conditional expectation, not a single endpoint {2024_Gao_FlowMatching}. The optimal-transport framing is likewise partial — the conditional path is the OT map between two Gaussians, but the learned marginal field is generally *not* an OT solution {2023_Lipman}{2022_Liu_MargOT}. Whether diffusion or flow matching is the better parameterization reduces to the unresolved weighting question of [[noise_schedules]] {2024_Gao_FlowMatching}. One-step distillation still trades quality or diversity for speed {2024_Liu_InstaFlow}.

## Synthesis

### From continuous normalizing flows to simulation-free training

A **continuous normalizing flow (CNF)** parameterizes a time-dependent velocity field $v(x,t)$ whose ODE $dx/dt = v(x,t)$ reshapes a simple prior $p_0 = \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ into the data distribution, and — being a neural ODE — admits exact likelihoods via the instantaneous change-of-variables $d\log p / dt = -\mathrm{tr}(\nabla v)$ {2019_Chen_NeuralODE}. But CNFs were impractical to train: maximum likelihood requires simulating the ODE, and simulation-free attempts hit intractable integrals {2023_Lipman}. **Flow matching** removes the simulation. Given a target probability path $p_t$ and a field $u_t$ that generates it, the objective is a simple regression $\mathbb{E}\,\| v(x,t) - u_t(x) \|^2$; since the marginal $u_t$ is unavailable, **conditional flow matching (CFM)** regresses instead onto a per-sample conditional field $u_t(x|x_1)$, which is closed-form, and the two objectives have identical gradients in $\theta$ {2023_Lipman}. This makes training a CNF as cheap and stable as training a diffusion model, without ever solving an ODE in the loop.

### Rectified flow and the optimal-transport view

Choosing the conditional path to be a straight line at constant speed, $x_t = t x_1 + (1-t) x_0$ with target velocity $x_1 - x_0$, gives **rectified flow** {2022_Liu_RectFlow}{2023_Lipman}. Two properties matter. First, the **reflow** procedure — retrain on deterministic $(x_0, x_1)$ pairs generated by the current model rather than random pairings — provably preserves the marginals while lowering transport cost and straightening trajectories at rate $O(1/K)$ in the number of reflow rounds, because a velocity field cannot point two ways at a path crossing and must untangle it {2022_Liu_RectFlow}. Second, the straight-line conditional path *is* the optimal-transport displacement between the two conditional Gaussians, so particles move in straight lines at constant speed {2023_Lipman}. The OT connection is only partial, though: rectified flow reduces cost for every convex cost $c$ without targeting any specific one, so a straight coupling is necessary but *not sufficient* for $c$-optimality, and a separate $c$-rectified variant is needed to actually solve a given OT problem {2022_Liu_MargOT}. Straightness matters computationally because a perfectly straight path is solved exactly in a single Euler step {2022_Liu_RectFlow}.

### Flow matching is diffusion with a particular schedule

The strongest organizing result is that flow matching and diffusion are **two views of one process** {2024_Gao_FlowMatching}. A diffusion forward process $z_t = a_t x_0 + s_t \epsilon$ reduces to the rectified-flow interpolation when $a_t = 1-t$, $s_t = t$; the DDIM sampler is invariant to a linear rescaling of $(a_t, s_t)$, so FM and variance-preserving diffusion trace the *same* reparameterized trajectory and differ only in weighting {2024_Gao_FlowMatching}. The velocity target $u = \epsilon - x_0$, the noise $\epsilon$, the clean $x_0$, the $v$-target, and the score are all linear reparameterizations of one another given $z_t$, so a diffusion score network can be converted to a flow field without retraining {2024_Gao_FlowMatching}{2025_Jin_AFloPS}. Two consequences correct common intuition: flow-matching *marginal* paths are actually curved, since the velocity returns the expected direction over the whole $\epsilon - x_0$ distribution rather than pointing at one endpoint; and neither schedule is uniformly straighter — VP is straighter for high-variance data, FM for low-variance data {2024_Gao_FlowMatching}. This places the whole framework inside the "everything is weighting" account of [[noise_schedules]] and the score/reverse-ODE account of [[score_matching]].

### Straightness, one-step generation, and its discontents

Because straight ODEs solve in few steps, flow matching is a natural route to **fast samplers** and **distillation**. InstaFlow turns Stable Diffusion into a one-step generator by applying reflow (to straighten) and only then distilling the straightened map into a single Euler step — reflow before distillation is essential, since distilling the raw curved model fails {2024_Liu_InstaFlow}. But the premise that straightness is what matters is directly challenged: **Rectified Diffusion** shows that reflow works on an ordinary DDPM keeping its noise schedule, $\epsilon$-prediction, and curved path, and argues the true target is a *consistent first-order ODE* (deterministic noise–data pairing so predictions stay constant along the trajectory), of which flow matching's straight path is only a special case {2024_Wang_RectDiffusion}. Under this reading, flow matching's linear interpolation and velocity-prediction are convenient but optional, and reflow is the load-bearing component. Fast training-free solvers reach the same trajectories from the other side: A-FloPS reparameterizes a diffusion model's score into flow-matching form and exploits the resulting temporal regularity for accurate few-step (5–10 NFE) sampling {2025_Jin_AFloPS}, part of the solver design space of [[diffusion_samplers]].

### Scaling to high-resolution text-to-image

Rectified flow is the training objective behind the current generation of large text-to-image models. **Stable Diffusion 3** pairs rectified flow with a multimodal diffusion transformer (MM-DiT) that keeps separate weights for text and image streams and mixes them only in attention, and finds — via a broad ablation of schedules and targets — that rectified flow *with a non-uniform (logit-normal) timestep sampling* that emphasizes the hard middle noise levels consistently beats $\epsilon$/$v$-prediction under cosine or linear schedules {2024_Esser_SD3}. The timestep distribution is exactly the loss-weighting knob of [[noise_schedules]]. **FLUX.1 Kontext** extends the same rectified-flow-in-latent-space recipe to in-context image generation and editing, concatenating reference-image and target tokens so one network handles text-to-image synthesis ($y = \text{Null}$) and image-conditioned local/generative editing ($y \neq \text{Null}$) under a single flow-matching loss {2025_BFL_FLUX} — a bridge to [[diffusion_based_image_editing]]. Both operate in the compressed autoencoder latent of [[latent_diffusion_models]].

### Beyond noise: bridges and general couplings

Flow matching's generality — any prior, not just Gaussian noise, and any coupling — connects it to **stochastic bridges**. Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models learn the score of a reverse-time diffusion *bridge* pinned between two arbitrary distributions via Doob's h-transform, unifying translation and generation; in the noiseless limit the bridge drift reduces exactly to the OT-flow-matching / rectified-flow velocity $x_1 - x_0$, and reduces to ordinary diffusion when the endpoint is Gaussian {2023_Zhou_DDBM}. This situates rectified flow as one point in a larger family that also includes Schrödinger bridges and stochastic interpolants, and explains why image-to-image translation, domain transfer, and generation are one problem under this lens {2022_Liu_RectFlow}{2023_Zhou_DDBM}. Training-side refinements target the same straightening goal from data: DANSM pre-pairs each sample with a geometrically "preferable" noise (minimizing path length, equivalently maximizing inter-path distance) so trajectories cross less and training converges faster, exploiting the same consistent-noise-pairing phenomenon that reflow discovers {2025_Xu_DANSM}.
